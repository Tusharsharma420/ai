# Customer Discovery Sprint

## Purpose

Choose a narrow first workflow for the Universal AI Workspace using evidence from real buyers and operators. This sprint validates the problem, not the popularity of AI agents.

**Do not build product code during this sprint.** Do not treat compliments, hypothetical interest, survey votes, or “AI is exciting” as proof of demand.

## Candidate workflows for the first comparison

Start with these three; the wider opportunity shortlist remains in `OPPORTUNITY_SHORTLIST.md`.

1. **LinkedIn agency delivery:** turn a client brief into research, content drafts, approval, publishing coordination, and performance reporting.
2. **B2B lead research and outbound preparation:** build a qualified prospect list, research accounts, prepare personalized drafts, and maintain CRM records.
3. **Small-business back-office operations:** complete a specific recurring admin workflow across records, spreadsheets, invoices, or reports.

These are candidates, not decisions. Narrow each to one repeated job during interviews.

## Who to interview

Seek people who personally performed the workflow in the last 30 days and can describe actual examples. Prioritize budget owners as well as hands-on operators.

| Candidate | Hands-on interviewees | Potential buyer |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn agency delivery | LinkedIn ghostwriters, personal-branding freelancers, small agency account managers | Agency founder or solo consultant |
| B2B outbound | SDR/BDR, lead-generation agency operator, founder doing outbound | Sales leader, agency owner, founder |
| Back-office | Small-business owner, office/admin manager, bookkeeper or operations lead | Owner or operations decision-maker |

Recruit **3–5 people per candidate initially** if reachable. This is directional discovery, not a statistically representative sample. Ask for referrals to people who do the work weekly.

## Short recruitment message

> Hey! I'm researching how [specific role/team] handles [specific workflow] today. I'm not pitching a product—I want to understand the real process, where time gets wasted, and what happens when things go wrong. Would you be open to a 15–20 minute conversation? I'll keep it focused on your actual experience.

Personalize the workflow and role. Do not mass-send identical messages or imply that a product already exists.

## Interview protocol (15–20 minutes)

### Opening
- “I'd like to understand how you do this today. There is no right answer, and I'm not selling anything.”
- Ask permission to take notes. Do not record without explicit consent.

### Understand the last real instance
1. “Tell me about the last time you completed [workflow]. When was it?”
2. “Can you walk me through every step from trigger to finished result?”
3. “Which tools, documents, people, and handoffs were involved?”
4. “Which step took the most time? Roughly how much time did the whole process take?”
5. “What went wrong most recently? What did the error or delay cost you?”
6. “How often does this happen, and who is responsible for the outcome?”
7. “What do you currently pay for tools, contractors, or staff to handle it?”
8. “What have you already tried to improve or automate? Why did it fail or fall short?”
9. “What information or actions must remain under human approval?”
10. “Who decides whether to buy a solution, and what would need to be true to justify the cost?”

### Test commitment, not compliments
- “Could we look at a sanitized example of the workflow or output?”
- “Would you be willing to try a manually delivered pilot on one real case?”
- If appropriate: “If it reliably achieved [specific outcome], what budget or existing expense could it replace?”
- Ask for a concrete next step: a follow-up with the budget owner, a sample, or a paid pilot discussion.

Do not lead with “Would you use an AI agent?” Do not promise full autonomy or claim integrations/capabilities that do not exist.

## Opportunity scorecard

Score each candidate from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Add a short evidence note for every score. Unknown is **unknown**, not a 3.

| Criterion | What a strong score means |
|---|---|
| Pain severity | The problem creates meaningful cost, risk, lost revenue, or delay |
| Frequency | The workflow repeats often enough to matter |
| Current spend | People already spend money or substantial staff time on it |
| Buyer access | We can reach users and decision-makers without a long sales cycle |
| Measurable outcome | Success can be measured in time, cost, quality, revenue, or turnaround |
| Pilot simplicity | We can deliver a useful result manually with little capital and limited integration |
| Repeatability | The same core workflow appears across multiple customers in the target segment |
| Trust/manageability | Data access, permissions, review, and failure recovery can be handled responsibly |

Do not rely on a single weighted total. Compare evidence quality, buying authority, and counter-evidence alongside scores.

## Initial decision gates

**Continue discovery** if there are credible recent examples of recurring pain but the buyer, budget, or solution boundary is still unclear.

**Run a concierge pilot** only when:
- Several independent interviewees describe a similar problem in their own words.
- The workflow and desired outcome can be stated precisely.
- A real user provides a sample, access to a real case, or another meaningful commitment.
- There is a plausible buyer and an explicit way to discuss payment.
- Risks and approval boundaries are understood.

**Do not build yet** if evidence consists mainly of hypothetical enthusiasm, generic AI interest, one unusually friendly contact, or problems without a reachable buyer.

A paid pilot is stronger evidence than verbal interest. If a pilot is unpaid, document why and set a clear conversion test before starting.

## First sprint checklist

- [ ] List 10 reachable people across the three candidate workflows.
- [ ] Send personalized invitations; track responses.
- [ ] Conduct the first 3 interviews without pitching.
- [ ] Write evidence and counter-evidence immediately after each interview.
- [ ] Refine the questions based on confusion, not on desired answers.
- [ ] Complete 3–5 initial interviews per candidate where access permits.
- [ ] Score candidates using evidence and mark unknowns explicitly.
- [ ] Choose one workflow for a concierge pilot—or decide that more discovery is needed.
- [ ] Update `DECISIONS.md` only when evidence supports a decision.

## Working principle

**Sell and deliver the outcome before building the platform.** Use people and existing tools behind the scenes to learn what must be automated, what must remain reviewable, and what buyers will actually pay for.
