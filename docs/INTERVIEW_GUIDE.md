# Customer Discovery Interview Guide

**Purpose:** Learn how a real workflow operates today and whether it is painful enough to justify a solution. This is not a product-pitch script.

## Before the interview
- Select a participant who personally performs, manages, or buys the workflow.
- Ask permission to take notes; do not collect secrets or sensitive customer data.
- Choose one specific workflow, not “AI adoption” in general.
- Record role, business type, team size range, and workflow context without unnecessary personal data.

## Opening
“I'm researching how teams handle [workflow]. I'm not trying to sell you a product today. I'd like to understand how it works in practice, including the annoying parts.”

## Core questions
1. Tell me about the most recent time you completed this workflow.
2. What triggered it? What was the desired end result?
3. Walk me through each step from start to finish.
4. Which tools, documents, systems, and people were involved?
5. Where did it slow down, fail, or require repeated manual work?
6. How often does this happen in a typical week or month?
7. How much staff time does one run take? How do you estimate that?
8. What happens when a step is missed or incorrect? Is there a financial, customer, or compliance impact?
9. What do you currently use to solve it? What have you tried and stopped using?
10. Have you paid for software, an agency, or staff time to improve this process? What did you pay for?
11. Who would decide whether to buy a solution, and which budget would it come from?
12. What would prevent you from trusting software to handle this workflow?
13. If we tested a small version with your existing tools, what would be a meaningful result?
14. Would you be willing to share a sanitized example, observe a run, or participate in a pilot? What approvals would be needed?

## Avoid leading questions
Avoid:
- “Would you use our universal AI Workspace?”
- “Wouldn't it save you lots of time?”
- “Would you pay ₹X for this idea?” before understanding current behavior and value.

Prefer asking about past behavior, current spend, existing workarounds, and a concrete next step.

## After each interview
Record:
- The workflow and trigger
- Current steps and systems
- Frequency and baseline time/cost (mark estimates clearly)
- Failure modes and consequences
- Existing alternatives and what is missing
- Buyer and purchase process
- Direct quotes only if accurate and permitted
- Evidence that contradicts our hypothesis
- Next action and confidence level

## Interview synthesis
Across interviews, look for repeated patterns in the same segment. Do not generalize from one vivid anecdote. If participants like the idea but will not share a workflow, introduce a pilot, or discuss purchase criteria, treat demand as unproven.
