# Roadmap and Phase Gates

This roadmap is evidence-driven. Add dates and engineering estimates only when scope and capacity are known.

## Phase 0 — Product discovery (current)
**Goal:** identify a painful, reachable, monetizable workflow.

Deliverables: candidate workflow inventory; customer-segment hypotheses; competitor/substitute map; source-backed research; customer/workflow evidence; ranked scorecard; documented beachhead decision.

**Exit gate:** a specific user and buyer, repeatable pain, credible evidence of current cost, and a feasible test.

## Phase 1 — Concierge validation
**Goal:** deliver the outcome manually or with existing tools before building a platform.

Deliverables: narrow workflow definition; baseline and target metrics; human-operated prototype; real workflow trial; failure/exception log; paid pilot or strong buying evidence where possible.

**Exit gate:** evidence of repeat usage/payment intent and measurable quality.

## Phase 2 — Thin workflow MVP
**Goal:** automate only proven repetitive steps.

Potential scope: basic workspace/task model; one workflow and limited integrations; one agent or deterministic automation where sufficient; approval UI; run history, errors, cost measurement, basic access controls.

**Exit gate:** reliable completion against agreed success thresholds and positive customer value.

## Phase 3 — Reusable platform primitives
Extract abstractions only after multiple workflows or customers demonstrate the need: workflow definitions, agent/tool registry, context retrieval, permissions, integrations, evaluations and audit logs.

**Exit gate:** reuse measurably reduces the cost/time of delivering the next workflow.

## Phase 4 — Productization and expansion
Improve onboarding, administration, reliability, billing, support, and deployment for the proven segment. Derive enterprise security, identity, audit, data residency, and compliance needs from actual customers.

## Stop or pivot criteria
- Users praise the idea but will not provide workflow access or commit to a pilot.
- The problem is infrequent or too inexpensive to solve.
- Existing tools solve it adequately at lower cost.
- Required reliability or permissions make economics unattractive.
- Each customer needs extensive custom work with little reusable value.
