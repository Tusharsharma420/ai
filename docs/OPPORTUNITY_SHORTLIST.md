# Initial Opportunity Shortlist

**Status:** Hypotheses for comparison, not recommendations  
**Date:** 2026-10-24

Do not choose the first use case based on personal excitement or platform breadth alone. Compare candidates using customer evidence, access to buyers, pain, willingness to pay, and time to prove value.

## Candidate A — LinkedIn personal branding agency delivery

**Potential buyer:** Founder, executive, consultant, or agency serving multiple personal-brand clients.  
**Workflow hypothesis:** Turn a client's goals and voice into a repeatable process for positioning, profile improvement, content, engagement, approvals, reporting, and renewal.

**Why investigate:** It is concrete and provides a workflow the team can potentially test through its own agency operations.  
**Risks:** Crowded market; public-facing content needs human review; engagement outcomes are noisy; many tools already support scheduling and writing.  
**Fast test:** Run one client workflow with existing tools and measure human hours, revision rate, delivery time, and willingness to pay.

## Candidate B — Lead research and personalized outbound operations for a small B2B agency

**Potential buyer:** Small B2B agency or founder-led sales team.  
**Workflow hypothesis:** Research a qualified account, verify relevant facts, prepare tailored outreach drafts, manage approval, and record follow-up state.

**Why investigate:** Repetitive research and handoffs may have measurable labor cost.  
**Risks:** Data quality, privacy, spam, platform terms, and deliverability. Sending must remain approval-gated; do not automate unsolicited sending without appropriate controls.  
**Fast test:** Prepare a small batch of research-backed drafts for a real sales team and measure research time, factual error rate, acceptance rate, and reply quality.

## Candidate C — Customer support triage and resolution preparation

**Potential buyer:** Small SaaS company or online business with recurring support requests.  
**Workflow hypothesis:** Classify incoming tickets, retrieve relevant policy and account context, draft a response or proposed action, and route uncertain/high-impact cases to a human.

**Why investigate:** Ticket volume, response time, and resolution quality can often be measured.  
**Risks:** Incorrect answers, privacy, account actions, and integration requirements.  
**Fast test:** Use a historical ticket set or shadow mode to compare triage accuracy, handling time, escalation quality, and unsupported claims.

## Candidate D — Internal SOP and knowledge-to-action workflow

**Potential buyer:** Small team with recurring processes documented across documents and chat.  
**Workflow hypothesis:** Answer a process request with sourced context, create the required task/artifact, and route it to the right owner for completion.

**Why investigate:** Knowledge retrieval can be paired with a measurable operational action rather than stopping at a text answer.  
**Risks:** Permission leakage, stale documents, unclear ownership, and overlap with existing collaboration tools.  
**Fast test:** Select one recurring internal process and compare time-to-completion, missed steps, and rework.

## Candidate E — Back-office operations for small businesses

**Potential buyer:** Small retailer, distributor, or service business.  
**Workflow hypothesis:** Coordinate a narrow process such as purchase-to-stock updates, invoice follow-up, or end-of-day reconciliation across existing tools.

**Why investigate:** Clear operational outcomes may create value beyond chat.  
**Risks:** Financial correctness, messy data, legacy workflows, and a wide range of business-specific needs.  
**Fast test:** Observe one business process end-to-end and map exceptions before proposing automation.

## How to rank the candidates

Score each from 1–5, but attach evidence and confidence:
- Pain severity and frequency
- Measurable time or financial cost
- Buyer and budget clarity
- Ability to reach 5–10 potential users
- Strength of existing alternatives
- Time to demonstrate value
- Technical and integration feasibility
- Risk and human-review burden
- Willingness to pay
- Reusability of the learning for the future platform

## Next step

Research each candidate, find actual prospective users, and document what they do today. Do not declare a winner until evidence is collected. A short paid pilot or credible commitment is stronger than enthusiastic feedback alone.
