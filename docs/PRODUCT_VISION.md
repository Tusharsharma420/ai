# Product Vision

**Status:** Hypothesis — not yet validated  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-24

## Vision

Create a configurable AI Workspace in which people and AI agents can work together to execute business processes with shared context, explicit responsibilities, controlled tool access, measurable outcomes, and human oversight.

The workspace could eventually support different organizations and operating models. A LinkedIn personal branding agency is one potential first-party use case, not the definition of the entire platform.

## Problem hypothesis

Businesses can access increasingly capable AI models, but turning model capability into dependable day-to-day work may still require people to manually connect tools, provide context, coordinate handoffs, review outputs, manage exceptions, and measure results.

This is a hypothesis to investigate, not a claim that every business experiences the same problem or will pay for a general-purpose platform.

## Potential value proposition

Help a team define a business outcome, break it into accountable work, route tasks to people or agents, provide the right context and tools, review important actions, and verify that the intended result was achieved.

The product should be evaluated on outcomes such as:
- Time from request to completed work
- Human review and correction time
- Completion and error rates
- Cost per successful outcome
- Visibility into status, ownership, and exceptions
- User trust and willingness to use it repeatedly

## Potential customer groups

These are research segments, not selected targets:
- Small businesses with repetitive operational workflows
- Agencies that deliver repeatable services to clients
- Startups and growing teams with cross-tool workflows
- Enterprises with governance, audit, identity, and compliance requirements

We should not assume one initial product can serve all segments equally well.

## Potential product capabilities

Only investigate these until a first use case is selected:
- Organization, workspace, role, and permission model
- Goals, projects, tasks, dependencies, and ownership
- Agent capability and instruction definitions
- Workflow orchestration, handoffs, retries, and exception queues
- Knowledge sources and context retrieval
- Tool and application integrations
- Approval gates and audit history
- Evaluation, observability, usage, and cost tracking
- Reusable templates for specific business workflows

## Non-goals for the discovery phase

- Building a universal agent builder before validating demand
- Supporting every industry and company size at launch
- Implementing a large microservices architecture prematurely
- Assuming multi-agent systems are better than a single agent or deterministic automation
- Automating public, financial, legal, or otherwise consequential actions without appropriate controls
- Building features only because competitors advertise them

## Strategic test

The product direction is promising only if research identifies a narrow workflow where:
1. The problem is frequent and costly enough to matter.
2. Existing tools or manual work leave a meaningful gap.
3. A buyer can be identified and has a plausible budget.
4. The result can be measured in a short pilot.
5. The solution can later share useful infrastructure with adjacent workflows.
